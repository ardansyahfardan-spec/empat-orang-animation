# Cinematography & Lighting

Use for camera setup, shot composition, lens choices, camera animation, lighting, exposure, and atmosphere.

## Camera rules
- Compose for vertical 9:16 first; protect faces and important action inside the safe frame.
- Choose shot size and angle for story purpose. Establish geography before close coverage.
- Motivate every camera move. Use push-ins, tracking, pans, tilts, handheld-like drift, and POV only when they improve the beat.
- Avoid arbitrary orbiting, excessive shake, repeated identical framing, and camera motion that hides weak acting.
- Maintain eyelines, screen direction, focus target, and spatial continuity across cuts.
- Use depth layers and foreground framing to create scale and suspense.

## Horror lighting
- Use motivated sources: moonlight, street lamps, practical bulbs, flashlights, windows, and interior spill.
- Shape readable silhouettes with controlled contrast, negative fill, rim light, haze, and selective pools of light.
- Keep faces and story-critical actions readable on mobile screens; darkness must not crush all detail.
- Let light changes support story events: flicker, practical lights turning on, flashlight sweeps, and controlled blackout.
- Use shadows and occlusion to imply presence before showing a threat.

## Technical checks
- Keep lighting deterministic at any timeline timestamp.
- Avoid expensive effects that cause unstable renders. Prefer efficient geometry, restrained volumetrics, and baked or procedural effects.
- Check clipping, flicker, z-fighting, exposure jumps, and composition at 1080x1920.
