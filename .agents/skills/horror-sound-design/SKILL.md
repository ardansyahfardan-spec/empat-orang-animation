# Horror Sound Design

Use whenever adding, editing, mixing, or reviewing music, ambience, dialogue, foley, or sound effects.

## Audio layers
1. Narration/dialogue: clear and intelligible, centered, with controlled dynamics.
2. Original score: tension beds, pulses, drones, restrained stingers, and emotional transitions.
3. Environment: night insects, wind, distant village ambience, room tone, and silence where useful.
4. Foley: footsteps matched to movement, clothing, gate/door sounds, floor creaks, object handling.
5. Horror accents: distant calls, whispers, low impacts, subtle risers, and carefully timed jumpscares.

## Mix rules
- Give every cue a timeline start, duration, gain, and fade.
- Duck music and ambience under narration. Keep dialogue intelligible on phone speakers.
- Use silence deliberately; do not fill every second with loud effects.
- Avoid clipping, harsh peaks, repetitive loops, and unrelated stock cues.
- Prefer original or properly licensed audio. Record source and license in project notes.
- Ensure all audio is seekable and synchronized with picture; avoid timing drift.
- Mix and export at a consistent sample rate, with sensible headroom and final loudness checks.

## Validation
Listen to the full episode and inspect waveform/peak levels. Verify no missing audio, abrupt loop seams, clipped peaks, or SFX that land on the wrong action.
