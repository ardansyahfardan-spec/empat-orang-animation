# Cinematic Storyboard Director

Use this skill whenever creating or revising an episode storyboard, shot list, scene timing, or edit.

## Required workflow
1. Read the episode story, character bible, current storyboard, and render specification before changing shots.
2. Break the story into numbered scenes and shots with explicit start/end times. The total timeline must cover the full episode; never pad missing visuals by freezing the last frame.
3. For every shot define: dramatic purpose, shot size, camera angle, camera movement, subject action, environment action, lighting cue, transition, and sound cue.
4. Use a deliberate shot grammar: establishing wide shots, medium two-shots, over-the-shoulder coverage, close-ups, inserts, reaction shots, and motivated POV shots.
5. Keep cuts purposeful. Use match cuts, motivated hard cuts, brief dissolves, or controlled black frames only when story supports them.
6. Build suspense through reveal timing, negative space, foreground obstruction, off-screen sound, and reaction shots. Do not reveal the ghost too early.
7. Prefer short, varied shots over long static holds. A camera move is not a substitute for character action.
8. Maintain screen direction, geography, eyelines, and continuity across adjacent shots.

## Output contract
Maintain `hyperframes-project/STORYBOARD.md` with a timed shot table. Every shot must map to an implemented scene/shot ID. Flag any shot not yet implemented rather than pretending it exists.

## EMPAT ORANG direction
Vertical 9:16, cinematic stylized 3D horror, Indonesian village atmosphere, grounded character drama, readable silhouettes, and suspenseful pacing. Preserve the four established character designs and story canon.
